# Toy examples

The examples in `ToyExamples.m2` are small, explicitly solved inputs for
testing rational univariate reconstruction. Every example consists of an
ideal $I$ of dimension $d$ in a polynomial ring with $n$ variables, together
with exactly $d$ affine hyperplanes. All defining equations and hyperplanes
have coefficients in $\mathbb{Q}$.

The hyperplanes cut $V(I)$ to a zero-dimensional scheme whose solutions are
known in advance. Rational coordinates are stored directly, while irrational
coordinates are stored numerically at 300-bit precision and accompanied by
exact descriptions such as $\sqrt{-2}$.

## Data format

Each entry of the `toyExamples` hash table has these fields:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `"Name"` | Stable name used as the key in `toyExamples` |
| `"n"` | Number $n$ of variables in the ambient polynomial ring |
| `"d"` | Dimension $d$ of the variety $V(I)$ |
| `"Ring"` | Polynomial ring over $\mathbb{Q}$ |
| `"Ideal"` | The dimension-$d$ ideal $I$ |
| `"Hyperplanes"` | Ideal generated by the $d$ slicing hyperplanes |
| `"SliceIdeal"` | The zero-dimensional slice ideal $I+H$ |
| `"KnownSolutionCoordinates"` | Coordinates used to construct the numerical points |
| `"KnownSolutionDescriptions"` | Exact human-readable coordinates |
| `"KnownSolutions"` | Numerical points at 300-bit precision |
| `"WitnessSet"` | Witness set constructed from the ideal, slice, and known points |

The current combinations of ambient dimension and variety dimension are:

| Name | $(n,d)$ | Degree | Number of slice points |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `nodal-cubic-rational-slice` | $(2,1)$ | $3$ | $3$ |
| `parabola-x2-plus-2` | $(2,1)$ | $2$ | $2$ |
| `two-irrational-lines` | $(3,1)$ | $2$ | $2$ |
| `quadratic-surface` | $(3,2)$ | $2$ | $2$ |
| `product-of-parabolas` | $(4,2)$ | $4$ | $4$ |

## Nodal cubic with a rational slice

Name: `nodal-cubic-rational-slice`

The ambient ring and ideal are

$$
R=\mathbb{Q}[x,y],
\qquad
I=\left\langle y^2-x^2(x+1)\right\rangle.
$$

Thus $n=2$ and $d=1$. The single slicing hyperplane is

$$
H=\left\langle 5y-18x+24\right\rangle.
$$

The three solutions of the sliced system are

$$
V(I+H)=
\left\{
(3,6),\ (8,24),\
\left(\frac{24}{25},-\frac{168}{125}\right)
\right\}.
$$

This example tests a singular curve while keeping all witness points rational
and away from the node $(0,0)$.

## Parabola with an irrational slice

Name: `parabola-x2-plus-2`

The ambient ring and ideal are

$$
R=\mathbb{Q}[x,y],
\qquad
I=\left\langle y-x^2\right\rangle.
$$

Here $n=2$ and $d=1$. The slicing hyperplane is

$$
H=\left\langle y+2\right\rangle.
$$

Substitution gives $x^2+2=0$, so

$$
V(I+H)=
\left\{
(\sqrt{-2},-2),\ (-\sqrt{-2},-2)
\right\}.
$$

This is the smallest example containing non-rational coordinates.

## Two irrational affine lines

Name: `two-irrational-lines`

The ambient ring and ideal are

$$
R=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z],
\qquad
I=\left\langle x^2+2,\ y-3\right\rangle.
$$

The ideal has $n=3$ and $d=1$. Over $\mathbb{C}$ its variety is the union of
the two lines with $x=\sqrt{-2}$ and $x=-\sqrt{-2}$. The slicing hyperplane is

$$
H=\left\langle z-5\right\rangle.
$$

The known solutions are

$$
V(I+H)=
\left\{
(\sqrt{-2},3,5),\ (-\sqrt{-2},3,5)
\right\}.
$$

This example checks a reducible positive-dimensional ideal.

## Quadratic surface

Name: `quadratic-surface`

The ambient ring and ideal are

$$
R=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z],
\qquad
I=\left\langle z^2+x+2\right\rangle.
$$

Here $n=3$ and $d=2$. The two slicing hyperplanes generate

$$
H=\left\langle x,y\right\rangle.
$$

The solutions are

$$
V(I+H)=
\left\{
(0,0,\sqrt{-2}),\ (0,0,-\sqrt{-2})
\right\}.
$$

This example exercises a two-dimensional variety and a codimension-two
rational slice.

## Product of two parabolas

Name: `product-of-parabolas`

The ambient ring and ideal are

$$
R=\mathbb{Q}[a,b,c,d],
\qquad
I=\left\langle c-a^2,\ d-b^2\right\rangle.
$$

Thus $n=4$ and $d=2$. The two slicing hyperplanes generate

$$
H=\left\langle c+2,\ d-3\right\rangle.
$$

The slice imposes $a^2+2=0$ and $b^2-3=0$. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(I+H)=\{&
(\sqrt{-2},\sqrt{3},-2,3),
&& (\sqrt{-2},-\sqrt{3},-2,3),\\
&(-\sqrt{-2},\sqrt{3},-2,3),
&& (-\sqrt{-2},-\sqrt{3},-2,3)
\}.
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the largest toy example and tests simultaneous irrational coordinates.

## Running the test loop

From the `src` directory, run:

```sh
M2 -q --stop -e 'load "HybridDecomposition.m2"; testToyExamples();'
```

For every example, the loop verifies that:

1. the coefficient ring is $\mathbb{Q}$ and the ring has $n$ variables;
2. $\dim(I)=d$;
3. there are exactly $d$ linear slicing equations;
4. $I+H$ is zero-dimensional and has the expected degree;
5. every recorded solution satisfies both the ideal and the hyperplanes;
6. `componentRepresentation` successfully reconstructs the RUR; and
7. solving $f(T)=0$ and evaluating
   $x_i=h_i(T)/(d_i f_{\mathrm{red}}'(T))$ recovers exactly the recorded
   solution set, up to numerical tolerance and independent of point ordering.

The loop prints the exact recorded solution set and the numerical solution set
recovered from the RUR at 20 displayed digits before performing the comparison.
